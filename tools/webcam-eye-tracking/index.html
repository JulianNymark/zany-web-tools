On-device · nothing is uploaded

Look where you look

Gaze estimation from an ordinary webcam. Calibrate to thirteen points, then pop a deterministic 5×5 grid of bubbles with your eyes — the same layout every run. Swap the tracker from the toolbar to compare.

Idle. The trackers load only when you press start; MediaPipe and clmtrackr are served from this site, WebGazer from jsDelivr.

How it works — and what it can’t know

Thirteen calibration points gather samples; a ridge regression fit in-page maps face features to screen coordinates. WebGazer.js skips that and trains its own regression from the same dots. Predictions are smoothed and drawn as a red gaze trace with per-sample dots. A bubble pops when your gaze dwells inside it.

  • Needs a secure context (HTTPS or localhost) and a served folder — file:// blocks the camera and WebAssembly.
  • Sit ~50–70 cm away, lit from the front, head fairly still. Expect centimetres, not millimetres.
  • Iris geometry alone is easy to bias: glasses, glare and any head shift after calibration show up as a constant offset. Leave Click to recalibrate on and every click quietly adds a sample where you were already looking.
  • The video never leaves the machine. MediaPipe and clmtrackr are served from this site; WebGazer is fetched from jsDelivr along with its face-mesh assets. The other network request is the optional live star count below.
Compare browser trackers

Three of these run here: MediaPipe Face Landmarker (neural net, WebAssembly, iris landmarks), clmtrackr (classic constrained local models, no ML, no iris) and WebGazer.js (TF.js + MediaPipe, self-trained from your calibration). The rest are external. Stars are pulled live from GitHub.
