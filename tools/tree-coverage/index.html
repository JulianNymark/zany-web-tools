How it's made — and what it can't know

Data & limits

  • Vector tiles from OpenFreeMap (OpenMapTiles schema), which stops at zoom 14. A z16/z18 square is therefore z14 geometry, overzoomed — small patches disappear.
  • There is no tree-canopy layer. A park counts as fully covered, so parks + woods is an upper bound and woods alone is the conservative tree-only number.
  • Individual street trees are not in the data at all.
  • Marine protected areas are in the park layer, so a point at sea can score “parks”.

Why these square sizes

There is no official size for a “neighbourhood”. The 3-30-300 methods review (Browning et al. 2024) defines it either by administrative units or by a buffer around the home, so the presets cover both: zoom squares for the first reading, fixed buffers for the second. Anchors: Perry's neighbourhood unit is ~160 acres (~800 m across), a 5-minute walk is ~300-400 m, and the Dutch average CBS buurt is ~244 ha (~1.6 km square).
