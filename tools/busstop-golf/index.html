Bus stop golf

How close together can two consecutive stops on an Oslo city bus line be? These are the 30 shortest hops, measured along the road the bus actually drives.

The leaderboard

    Pick any two stops

    Pick stop A — stop B then offers only the stops connected to it on some line. In a line's middle you get two neighbours, at an end just one.

    Where you are

    Track your position live: when you come within 100 m of either end of a top-30 pair, the card pops and the walk is drawn.

    How it's made — and what it can't know

    Data

    • Stops, lines and drive-path geometry from Entur's JourneyPlanner v3 (Ruter's Oslo/Akershus network). Free, open data.
    • Only regular city lines count: 1–3-digit Ruter route numbers, both stops inside the city. Regional 4-digit routes (school runs out in Romerike) are excluded — that's where the silly 2 m "winners" lived.
    • Distance = length of the line's own drive-path polyline between the two stops (quay to quay, direction-true), computed from the geometry — the API's length field is unreliable.
    • 257 unique consecutive pairs across 205 stops, generated 26 September 2026.

    Limits

    • Distances are as the bus drives, not as you walk — your footpath may be shorter.
    • Some short pairs are hubs split across a street (Carl Berners plass, Jernbanetorget): real stops, same place to a human.
    • Geolocation needs HTTPS and your permission; nothing is stored anywhere — position never leaves the page.
